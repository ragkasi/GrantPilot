HORIZON YOUTH COLLECTIVE
Mission Statement and Organizational Overview
========================================================

MISSION

Horizon Youth Collective is a 501(c)(3) nonprofit organization dedicated to
empowering low-income middle and high school students in Columbus, Ohio through
hands-on STEM education, near-peer mentoring, and career exploration. We believe
that access to quality STEM programming should not depend on a student's zip code
or household income.

ORGANIZATIONAL HISTORY

Horizon Youth Collective was founded in 2019 by a group of STEM educators and
community advocates who saw a persistent gap in out-of-school STEM opportunities
for students in under-resourced Columbus neighborhoods. We launched our first
after-school robotics cohort in September 2019 with 18 students and two volunteer
mentors. By 2024, we served 112 students annually across three program sites.

TAX-EXEMPT STATUS

Horizon Youth Collective received its IRS 501(c)(3) determination in March 2020
(EIN: 88-0000000). We are in good standing with the IRS and the State of Ohio.
Our most recent Form 990 was filed for fiscal year 2024. We operate on a
July 1 – June 30 fiscal year.

ANNUAL BUDGET

Our fiscal year 2025 operating budget is $485,000. Revenue sources include:
- Foundation grants: 62%
- Individual donations: 21%
- Government contracts: 12%
- Program fees (subsidized): 5%

We maintain a 90-day operating reserve and undergo an annual financial review
by an independent CPA.

POPULATION SERVED

We serve students in grades 6–12 who meet at least one of the following criteria:
- Household income at or below 185% of the federal poverty line
- Enrolled in a Title I school
- Identified as first-generation college-going

In fiscal year 2024:
- 112 students served
- 94% qualify for free or reduced-price lunch
- 78% are students of color
- 61% female or non-binary
- 100% reside in Franklin County, Ohio

PROGRAMS

After-School STEM Cohort (grades 6–8)
Students meet three afternoons per week, 3:30–6:00 PM, from October through May.
Programming includes robotics (VEX IQ), data science projects, environmental
science investigations, and college preparation workshops. Each cohort of 20
students is supported by two paid STEM coaches and four college-student mentors.
Average attendance rate: 87%.

Summer STEM Intensive (grades 9–11)
A 6-week summer program providing 8 hours per day of project-based STEM
learning. In 2024, 32 students completed 240 hours each of STEM instruction.
Topics included Python programming, engineering design challenges, and a
capstone community impact project.

OUTCOMES

Over our first five program years:
- 96% of students in grades 8–11 report increased interest in STEM careers
- 88% of eligible alumni enrolled in college (vs. 45% district average)
- 14 alumni are currently pursuing STEM degrees
- Students earned 23 regional science fair awards

PARTNERSHIPS

- Columbus City Schools (formal MOU for student recruitment and space)
- Ohio State University College of Engineering (mentors and facility access)
- Columbus Metropolitan Library (co-location for two program sites)
- TechColumbus (employer partners for career days)

STAFFING

Executive Director: 1 FTE
Program Director: 1 FTE
STEM Coaches: 2 FTE
AmeriCorps Members: 2 (part-time)
College Mentors: 8–12 (volunteer, academic year)
